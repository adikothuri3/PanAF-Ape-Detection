# 2026-07-27 — Check-in

## The headline

**A larger pretrained variant nearly doubled detection recall for one line of configuration.**
`MDV6-yolov10-e` against the `MDV6-yolov9-c` we had been using, same 10 clips, same threshold:

| | yolov9-c | yolov10-e |
|---|---|---|
| Precision | 0.874 | 0.862 |
| **Recall** | **0.386** | **0.745** |
| F1 | 0.536 | **0.799** |

Precision held — normally recall is bought by accepting more false boxes, and here it was not. Cost:
121 s instead of 99 s for the whole sample on an A100. No training.

Tracking coverage doubled as a consequence, 0.351 → 0.728, and individuals we essentially lost fell
from 9 to 2 of 23.

**What this answers.** Phase 1 exists to decide whether the gap is *capacity* or *domain mismatch*.
It was **capacity**. That is a real result, and it argues *against* fine-tuning rather than for it.
Plain-English write-up: [variant comparison](../../../reports/variant_comparison_2026-07-27.md).

## Results, at confidence 0.20

| | Precision | Recall | F1 | Pooled mean IoU |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Detector only | 0.874 | 0.386 | 0.536 | 0.825 |
| + ByteTrack | 0.917 | 0.353 | 0.509 | 0.832 |

Tracking: 23 annotated individuals, 29 predicted tracks, 17 ID switches, mean fragmentation 1.35,
coverage 0.353, 7 mostly-tracked / 9 mostly-lost.

**The interesting part is what the full sample overturned.** The 3-clip write-up reported `hanging`
recall at 0.000 and called it blindness; over 1346 instances it is 0.102. It also showed a
"large subject dip"; with 10 clips that is a composition artifact — in the two clips containing large
*and* small subjects together, large-subject recall is 0.856 and 0.899, the best of any band. The
real common factor in every failing clip is **low contrast**: a dark animal against a background of
similar luminance, whether the frame is dark, infrared, or backlit and blown out. Read the next
section before drawing a conclusion from any of these numbers.

## The threshold sweep — the actual headline

I nearly sent this check-in saying "MegaDetector fails on low-contrast footage". That would have been
wrong, and expensively so.

| Confidence | Precision | Recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0.05** | 0.628 | **0.563** | **0.594** |
| 0.20 *(what everything above was measured at)* | 0.874 | 0.386 | 0.536 |
| 0.50 | 0.943 | 0.280 | 0.432 |

F1 rises the whole way down and **has not turned over at 0.05**. The recovered detections are not
spread evenly — they land almost entirely on the clips I had written off:

| Clip | R@0.20 | R@0.05 |
|---|---|---|
| `isfRigsIjO` (near-dark) | **0.000** | 0.433 |
| `XmOoOk9n7t` (backlit) | 0.361 | 0.892 |
| `z97mIEQzcL` (deep shade) | 0.044 | 0.492 |
| `RHH9DDfWZa` (infrared) | 0.009 | 0.265 |

`hanging` goes 0.102 → 0.527. **The detector was not blind to these apes — it was scoring them
between 0.05 and 0.20, and the threshold was throwing them away.**

Getting that number required finding a bug: `det_conf_thres` was never passed to
PyTorch-Wildlife, so every run in the repo's history inferred at the library's 0.2 default whatever
the config said. Because the configured value was *also* 0.2, every published number is still
correct — but a sweep below 0.2 was silently impossible, and the first attempt returned output
byte-identical to the 0.20 run. That identity is the only symptom; nothing raises.

This is the **second** PyTorch-Wildlife parameter that is accepted and then not applied, after
`device=`. Both are now documented in [[model]] with the rule I have taken from them: in this
library, an argument being accepted is no evidence it is applied — verify by observing behaviour.

## The catch — and a question it raises

I expected tracking at 0.05 to give the best of both: more recall, with `minimum_track_length`
filtering the noise. It does not. Tracking the 0.05 detections gives **identical coverage to 0.20**
and slightly *more* ID switches.

`sv.ByteTrack` has two hardcoded thresholds, neither configurable:

```python
inds_low = scores > 0.1                                    # <= 0.1 discarded outright
self.det_thresh = self.track_activation_threshold + 0.1    # new tracks need activation + 0.1
```

So nothing scoring 0.1 or below can ever start a track — and in the hardest clip, **87% of the
recovered detections score at or below 0.1**. The detections that rescue the low-contrast footage are
exactly the ones the tracker refuses to use.

I had assumed setting `track_activation_threshold` from our confidence threshold made the tracker
consider everything the detector kept. It does not, and our `bytetrack.py` said so in a docstring —
now corrected. That is the third parameter today that is accepted and does not mean what its name
implies; the first two were upstream's, this one I built on top of.

## Questions

- **Is ByteTrack still the right tracker?** The threshold question answered itself — yolov10-e peaks
  at the default 0.20, so the mistuned operating point was a symptom of the smaller model. But ID
  switches went 19 → 46 with the better detector, and identity stability is what Phase 2 pose
  estimation actually needs. Worth swapping the tracker, or is tuning it enough?
- **Does this change what Phase 1 "done" means?** The write-up describing `MDV6-yolov9-c` is now a
  description of a model we no longer use. I have kept it and cross-referenced it rather than
  rewriting history, but if you would rather the headline write-up describe the current model, say
  so and I will restructure.
- **Is contrast preprocessing still worth doing?** Almost certainly not now. The low-contrast clips
  it was aimed at — infrared, deep shade, backlit — reach 100% recall with the larger model.
- **Is 10 purposively-hard clips the right sample to stop at**, or is a random sample of similar size
  worth adding for contrast? Everything measured so far describes these clips, not PanAf500, and I
  would rather say that clearly than quietly generalise.
- Carried forward: the [[Reading List]] ambiguity (the onboarding asks for "the two *Our world*
  items" but lists one), and the missing [[SPARROW]] and [[DimensionalOS dimos]] URLs.

## Next

1. `MDV6-yolov10-e` is now the project default, and all 10 clips are being re-run from it so the
   annotated videos and headline numbers come from the model we actually recommend.
2. **Tracking is now the bottleneck, not detection.** Coverage doubled, but ID switches went 19 → 46,
   because more apes on screen means more chances to swap two of them. The free experiments come
   first: ByteTrack activation at 0.20 rather than 0.05 (already measured better on every axis) and a
   lower `minimum_track_length`, both re-run over saved detections in seconds.
3. Then Phase 2 (pose), which is more valuable now that we follow 73% of each ape's screen time
   rather than 35%.

**Fine-tuning has moved down, not up.** A config change closed most of the gap; what remains would
need the real PanAf500 train split, not our 10 evaluation clips, and the cheap tracking levers are
still untouched.
